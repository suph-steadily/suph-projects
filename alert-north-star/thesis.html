The Dwelling Age Thesis

Do the underwriter's four jobs automatically, exactly the way they do them. Then turn the alert off. If the duplication is faithful, nothing bad happens — and about 115 policies a month stop dying at a roadblock.

This page is the worked example: dwelling age is the first alert pushed through the playbook. Here is what's measured, what's missing, and what happens next.

Step 2 · Flip the switch

What we expect when the alert goes dark

The upside
  • +115 bound policies / month (range +50 to +175)
  • 2,200 desk reviews / month eliminated
  • $310K / yr of UW premium corrections kept — automated, not lost
What must hold
  • NOCs: ±0 — raw removal would add ~25/mo (4.5 → 6.5 per 100)
  • UW corrective endorsements: ±0 — raw removal would add ~30/mo (3.9 → 7.5 per 100)
  • Loss ratio: unknown — the weight the machine still can't price

All rates here are per 100 bound policies within 90 days of bind, so 4.5 per 100 means 4.5%. Why ±0 is credible: the un-gated twin gets about 2 extra NOCs per 100 — and roughly 1.5 of those are things the four jobs cover (roof, eligibility, data). The last half comes from what the on-site inspection finds, which the review doesn't prevent today either. The honest caveat: without the jobs, the true un-reviewed rate projects to 7–11 per 100, because the 101+ book averages 117 years old and risk ramps +22.8% per decade.

Step 3 · The honest part

The leftover NOCs don't go away — and they were never the alert's to catch

Even with an underwriter reviewing every single bind, the reviewed book still runs 4.5 NOCs and 3.9 corrective fixes per 100. Duplicating the underwriter keeps that rate. Here's why the review can't see it coming:

This is the next frontier, not a reason to keep the alert. ~60% of the leftover NOCs are general-condition findings, ~20% liability hazards (trampolines, animals, structures). Shrinking them means getting condition information before bind: imagery at quote time, attestation questions. Worth doing whether or not the alert lives.

One exception proves the roof lever: of the 12 roof-condition NOCs on the reviewed book, zero had the roof exclusion at bind. The one lane the review could have caught, it skipped — and the dial fixes exactly that.

Step 4 · The playbook

Order of operations, for this alert and every one after

Dwelling age is the first pass. Every later alert reruns the same loop, cheaper, because the machine and the levers are reusable.

  1. Set the tolerance once.

    A named unit: uncured cancellations per 100 binds, 90 days. 74% of NOCs cure, so "letters sent" and "policies lost" differ 4x. Without this number the machine outputs a price with no verdict.

  2. Pick the alert.

    By forced-review volume. Dwelling age is #1: 5,044 underwriter touches a month.

  3. Weigh it.

    The sister cohort plus the machine: binds gained, NOCs and fixes added, dollars either way. Already under tolerance? Skip straight to the 10% test.

  4. Learn the underwriter's jobs on that alert.

    Action mix, what they change, what the leftover NOCs are for. Done for dwelling age: 46.5 / 25.3 / 28.2 / 8.4.

  5. Systematize the jobs.

    Roof dial and pre-fill sourcing are ready to start; attestations next; Spotlight signals ~October; auto-approve comes free at the end.

  6. Prove it with the alert still on.

    Zero risk: the gate still catches everything. Two gauges, not one: rubber-stamp climbs from 46.5% toward 80-90%, and underwriters stop overriding what the robot did. Fewer touches plus no corrections of the robot = faithful duplication.

  7. Turn it off for 10%.

    Automation only defends the NOC rate; the 63 who die at the roadblock come back only when the alert itself disappears. Verify the right rejections still happen (82% of real "no"s are eligibility) and NOC and fix rates hold at reviewed-book levels. Kill criteria agreed up front; NOCs need ~90 days to read.

  8. Re-weigh with real data. Ramp.

    The 10% test replaces the sister-cohort estimate: measured, not projected. Present against the tolerance, then 10 → 50 → 100.

  9. Log it. Loop.

    Keep watching the leftover NOC rate. Pick the next alert and rerun from step 3.
